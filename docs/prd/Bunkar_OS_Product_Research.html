How a Bhadohi carpet unit works, step by step

Most features only make sense once you know the journey of one carpet. A hand-knotted carpet takes about 3 to 6 months on the loom. Wool goes out by the kilo, often through a contractor to a loom in a village, and comes back months later as a finished carpet that is measured, weighed and paid for. Money, wool and responsibility change hands at every step, and today all of it is written in paper registers.

Built from our Phase 1 visits and the research report. Steps marked "to check" still need confirming with our design partners.

01 · What we are building

Product vision

Bunkar OS is a Hindi, phone-first, offline-capable app for the loom-to-exporter chain of a small Bhadohi carpet unit. It tracks every carpet from wool issue, through a village loom (often through one or two thekedars), bazaar (the day a finished carpet comes off the loom and is measured, weighed and paid for), washing and finishing, to delivery to the exporter. It answers the three questions Phase 1 owners asked most:

  • Where is my wool? Kg issued compared with kg that came back as carpet, checked at the bazaar rather than at year-end.
  • What does each weaver and thekedar really owe or get? Wages by area, advances (peshgi) and deductions, with photo proof and the weaver's own acknowledgement.
  • When will this order ship? Progress from real loom check-ins, which can be shared with the exporter.

Because every entry records actual work, the unit also builds up the records its exporter needs at almost no extra effort: production-site lists, wage proof, age checks and per-carpet history. Under GoodWeave these duties fall on the licensed exporter (GoodWeave P08 v5.2). Supplier units are where the data comes from. Buyers such as IKEA (IWAY) audit down to subcontractors. The EU Forced Labour Regulation applies from December 2027 (Covington; date not re-checked in this round).

Who uses it

User What they do in the app What they get
Owner (malik) Reads the 8 pm digest, locks the day's entries, approves rates, advances, write-offs and deletions, and shares status with the exporter The full picture while away: orders at risk, wool and money held outside, dues, and entries by staff
Munshi / staff Enters yarn issue, bazaar, payments, roker and stock in Hindi by tap, photo or (later) voice, offline One record instead of register plus Excel, fewer arguments, and entries that are locked and traceable
Thekedar / sub-thekedar Posts loom check-ins and problems for his own looms by WhatsApp photo or a light app, and receives yarn, advances and wage funds A clear ledger of what he was given, returned and is owed, at each tier
Weaver (bunkar) Gets a Hindi slip (text and voice) after each bazaar or payment, agrees or disputes by tap or missed call, and reports loom problems by voice note His own record of rate, area, deductions, advance and net pay, and a voice in disputes
Exporter (the unit's buyer) Opens a status link for each order without logging in. Later, sees all its supplier units in one portal. Loom photos, % woven, stage and estimated date, plus site lists and age checks for compliance, without phoning around

Core insight: how we stand out

  1. Against SME wage and ledger apps (PagarBook, SalaryBox, OkStaff, Vyapar, Khatabook):
    • These apps assume SKUs and daily or monthly salaries.
    • Carpets are one-of-a-kind pieces paid by area and quality. Wool goes out by kg and comes back as a carpet also weighed in kg. Peshgi runs for months.
    • Play Store reviewers say PagarBook struggles with per-piece weekly pay and carrying advances forward, and that SalaryBox lacks per-piece entry.
    • Our core is carpet logic (kg to sq ft, area and quality to wage), not general bookkeeping.
  2. Against carpet ERPs (JP Software, VNV, ERPDaddy, jet66, Digify):
    • Their feature coverage is not our advantage. JP's site already lists bazaar entry, pending material at loom, loom visit ("daura") entry, loom balance reports, measurement registers, contractor and finisher payment ledgers, carpet-number barcodes, dyer billing and an export section.
    • JP is also listed as cloud-based plus desktop, with Android and iPhone apps.
    • We win on who can enter data (munshi, thekedar and weaver, in Hindi, by voice or photo, offline, from the village) and who can see it (the owner while away, the weaver on his own slip, the exporter through a link).
    • These ERPs have almost no public reviews and all price on request.
  3. Against how large firms do it:
    • Big exporters run loom visibility with people. Jaipur Rugs works with 28,000 artisans (BCtA case study; AIM2Flourish says 40,000 in 600 villages). It uses an ERP plus Salesforce CRM, sends QC inspectors to looms and pays artisans monthly.
    • Obeetee works with 30,000+ weavers in 900 villages (Forbes India).
    • Small units cannot afford field staff or in-house IT. Our bet is to give a 20-loom Gopiganj unit the loom-level visibility and wage records large exporters get from inspectors and ERPs, through the people already in the chain.
  4. The trust layer is part of the product. The top one-star themes in Indian SME app reviews are:
    • paywalls added after users formed habits
    • data held hostage
    • broken offline mode
    • support that disappears after payment

Section 4 answers these with trust promises (flat pricing, data export, offline entry, a named local onboarding person) and with features (audit log, daily lock, backup).

03 · Stand-out bets
# Bet Features Why it wins (honestly) How to validate with the 5 design partners
1 Wage truth: rate card, bazaar, deductions, peshgi, and a slip the weaver can hear C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, C8, C9, C10 The strongest bet. No carpet ERP found sends a slip to the weaver with agree/dispute. Generic wage apps fail at piece rates and advances (PagarBook and SalaryBox Play reviews). Voice and IVR reach weavers who cannot read or have no smartphone. Risk: some owners may not want weavers to see deductions, because opacity is bargaining power. First, ask owners whether they would send slips. Then send text and voice slips to 20+ weavers across 3 units for 4 weeks. Success: at least 70% delivered and heard or opened, disputes logged in the app rather than argued face to face, and the owner reports fewer wage-count arguments than in Phase 1.
2 Tauli at the bazaar: variance caught on the spot, not at year-end E1, E2, K3 JP and VNV already track pending material and wool requirement, so the concept is not new. What is new: the check runs on a phone at the moment of bazaar, with a moisture-aware norm, a cause code and the weaver's acknowledgement. Collect gm/sq ft norms from partners. Record 4 weeks of issues and bazaars at 3 units. Success: at least one above-tolerance variance per unit that the owner agrees is real, few false alarms in monsoon, and the owner keeps the alert on.
3 Everyone in the chain can enter data: Hindi, photo, voice, offline, with the register retired B2, M1, M2, M3, O1, N1, N3 Carpet ERPs are built for office operators. Vyapar or Khatabook could add Hindi voice. Success: after 4 weeks the munshi stops writing the paper roker at at least 2 units, at least 2 thekedars post one check-in a week unprompted, and the owner locks the day at least 5 days in 7.
4 Exporter channel: status link plus compliance output (B2B2B) A2, J1, J2, J3, J6 GoodWeave P08 duties (immediate new-site reporting, 6-monthly site lists, label records) fall on licensed exporters. Today they gather this from suppliers by hand. If one exporter asks its supplier units to use Bunkar OS, it gets the data automatically, and we get a distribution channel. RMPro shows rug vendors share progress with buyers. Risks: it competes with GoodWeave's own platform and exporters' ERPs, and only works if an exporter signs on. Show a site list and status link built from real partner data to 2 exporters that supply GoodWeave or IKEA buyers. Success: one exporter agrees to be a channel partner or to request the data from its suppliers.
5 Multi-tier thekedar layer with opt-in pass-through D1, D2, D3, C5 JP has single-tier contractor payments, so "nobody models contractors" would be false. What is new: sub-thekedar tiers, rates at each tier, thekedar peshgi, reassignment, and (opt-in) what actually reached the weaver. PARI (Mirzapur) documents the gap: Rs 2,200 to the thekedar vs Rs 1,200 to the weaver. Risk: thekedars will resist D2, so expect it only where an exporter requires it. Demoted from the draft: "Order-hold and WIP-at-risk" is now features A3 and I2 (P2), not a bet. The 2025 tariff shock may be easing (ibcworldnews, Feb 2026). Register-photo OCR is no longer a headline claim: K5 is manual first.

Commercial validation: we have no verified evidence yet that Bhadohi manufacturing units pay for software. Modern Tek's Rs 45,000 tool is retail software. Reference prices for SME tools are VastraApp Rs 5,000 a year, PagarBook Rs 499-1,398 per staff per year, TranZact from Rs 9,000 a quarter and the Tally job-work add-on Rs 13,500. Before building P1, ask each partner for a paid pilot commitment at a stated flat per-unit price.

08 · What not to build
Don't build Why
Full accounting or GST billing Tally, Vyapar and myBillBook own this, and CAs expect Tally. Export instead (P1 feature).
Design CAD or CAD import Galaincha and NedGraphics are entrenched and their formats are proprietary. Design can be outsourced (Fiverr from $50). Store and route maps (F1, F2); don't draw them.
IoT loom sensors Village hand looms have no power (Trackton is for powerlooms). Photo check-ins are the realistic sensor.
Lending or loans in the app Khatabook's loan complaints and permission issues hurt trust, and its lending struggled on ledger data quality (The Morning Context)
Marketplace or idle-loom exchange Fashinza shows how exposed marketplaces are to demand cycles. Out of scope.
D2C / Etsy listing tools A different business from supplying exporters
"GI certificates" Only authorised users under the registered GI may use the tag. Generating certificates is legally risky. Record the authorised-user number instead (H3).
SKU reorder-point inventory as the core One-of-a-kind rugs have no SKU (capitalandgrowth). Piece identity, not reorder logic.
Biometric or selfie attendance Weavers work on home looms and are paid by output, not attendance
A generic "WhatsApp AI agent" as the headline Digify already markets one. WhatsApp is the channel, not the differentiator.
A "100 reports" back office JP's pitch. Show exceptions and a digest instead.
Per-user, per-order or per-SKU pricing, or retroactive caps A recurring one-star theme across every category
Full offline conflict handling, hash-chained logs or WhatsApp Business API in P0 Too big for 2 developers in a prototype. Use the append-only queue, a plain audit table and wa.me share instead.
Custom per-client builds The Atna/D365 customisation trap. Configure through rate cards, deduction codes and process routes instead.
09 · Open questions for design partners
  1. Wage transparency: will owners send weavers a slip that shows deductions and advance balance? Will thekedars allow D2 pass-through visibility, and under what exporter pressure?
  2. Weaver phones: what share of weavers have their own smartphone, a keypad phone or a shared family phone? Is voice or IVR (C9/C10) needed from day one?
  3. Script: do munshis in Gopiganj and Aurai prefer Hindi, Hinglish or Urdu script? Should Urdu move into P1?
  4. Tauli norms: what gm/sq ft norms per quality, allowed wastage and monsoon moisture tolerance do owners use today? Will they share them?
  5. Thekedar chain: how many tiers (thekedar, sub-thekedar, family head) are typical? Is each tier paid a different rate? How often does a thekedar leave with a half-woven piece?
  6. Thekedar behaviour: will thekedars post weekly photo check-ins unprompted, or only if paid or asked by the owner?
  7. Customer mix: what share of partner output goes to Bhadohi exporters vs direct foreign buyers? This decides whether multi-currency and export documents matter.
  8. Exporter channel: which exporters (GoodWeave, IKEA or Care & Fair suppliers) would pilot the status link and site list? What do they currently ask suppliers for?
  9. Cash and terms: what exporter payment terms (critic suggests 60-120 days, unverified) and rejection or debit-note rates do units see?
  10. Roker and register: what exactly does the munshi's roker contain today? What would let the owner stop the paper register?
  11. Finishing: does the unit finish in-house or through vendors? How much shrinkage or weight loss after washing changes invoice sq ft?
  12. Multi-firm and GST: how many owners run several firms or family-name entities? Are small units GST-registered, and do they file ITC-04?
  13. Samples: how many samples a year, at what absorbed cost, and what share converts to bulk?
  14. Pricing: what flat per-unit monthly or yearly price will partners commit to for a paid pilot?
  15. Evidence gaps to close: re-capture the PagarBook and SalaryBox Play Store reviews with dates. Find a primary source for Jaipur Rugs' field tools if we want to cite them. Record review sample sizes before quoting percentages.
